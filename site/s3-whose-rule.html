Whose rule is it?

Trent called from a job: “The thermostat, TH-7710, shows up as backordered, so I almost drove across town for one. The supply house says it's on the shelf.”

You check: the catalog data is right. TH-7710 is in stock, and the card even says so. BUT the part card has an orange stripe (on the left) which signifies ‘not in stock’. Barf: the view is wrong. Your job is to find out why using your browser's developer tools on this page.

At HinH, parts.css is a component of their design system: any parts-related page uses it, it changes a few times a year, and the pages change nearly every week. So the pages depend on the design system, never the other way around.

Be ready to explain
  1. Which rule makes TH-7710 look backordered (adds the orange stripe), and where it lives (file and line). A rule is one entry in a stylesheet: a selector, like .catalog-part, followed by the properties it sets in { }. In the Styles pane, each block is one rule.
  2. Why that rule won over the other rules that also style the card.
  3. The one-line change you'd commit.
Opening the developer tools

Use Chrome or another Chromium browser (Edge, Brave, Arc). Right-click the TH-7710 card and choose Inspect, or press Cmd+Option+I on a Mac (Ctrl+Shift+I on Windows).

In the Elements panel, the card's HTML is highlighted. The Styles pane next to it lists every rule that applies to that element, the winner on top. A rule with a line through it lost. Each rule shows the file and line it came from on its right.

You can click any value in Styles and change it, or untick it. That changes this tab only. Nothing is saved.

Safari works too, after you turn on Settings → Advanced → “Show features for web developers.” Ad blockers and password managers sometimes add their own errors and elements to the tools. That's normal; ignore anything that isn't about this page.

Fix it in the tools, then check

  1. Inspect the TH-7710 card. In Styles, find the rule that makes it look backordered (adds the orange stripe).
  2. Change it right there in the tools until TH-7710 looks like the other in-stock parts, and nothing else on the page changes.
  3. Click Check the page. It looks at the page as it is in your tab right now.
  4. Then reload the page. Watch what happens to TH-7710.
